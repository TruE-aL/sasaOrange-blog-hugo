{{/* The loading sheet for in-page navigation: two opaque halves of paper that close over the screen and draw back again. It lives outside #page-shell, and that is the whole trick. In-page navigation replaces the shell, so a sheet inside it would be torn away the moment the new page arrived — mid-motion, with the opening half-seen. Out here the sheet outlives every shell it covers: the new page is laid in behind it, and only when it is in place does the paper draw back. What the opening reveals is therefore always the page the reader asked for, never the one they were leaving. That is reimu's own order of operations, and it is why the sheet is rendered after the shell instead of inside it. The motion is reimu's, not a generic spinner's: two opaque halves of paper slide in from the sides and close over the whole screen, and slide apart again when the next page is ready — the header rides on top of them the whole time, still clickable. The mark in the middle is reimu's taiji's place, taken by a cog. The close is given its full duration before the page is laid in (ruri.js), the way reimu gives its loader a minimum showing, so a fast navigation — a cache hit — reads as a gesture rather than a flicker. None of it may take a click, a focus ring or a layout slot of its own — the reader must stay able to cancel by clicking the page, and the live region is kept out of the accessibility tree while the veil is hidden, so the message is announced once — when the veil opens — instead of on every page load. */}}
